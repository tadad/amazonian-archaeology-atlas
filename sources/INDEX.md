# Source Library

This index privileges primary sources, excavation/field reports, critical
editions, and strong open-access scholarship. A local link means a copy was
downloaded for research continuity; the linked holding institution remains the
preferred citation target. File checksums and sizes are in
[`DOWNLOAD-MANIFEST.md`](DOWNLOAD-MANIFEST.md).

Searchable Markdown copies of the local PDFs, with OCR and conversion caveats,
are tracked in [`markdown/INDEX.md`](markdown/INDEX.md).

## Indigenous and colonial Andean sources

### Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala, *Nueva corónica y buen gobierno* (1615/1616)

- **Type:** autograph Indigenous-authored colonial manuscript; primary
  ethnohistorical and visual source.
- **Coverage:** Inca dynastic traditions, ritual, calendrics, burials, colonial
  administration, roads/tambos, and a sustained Indigenous critique of colonial
  rule.
- **Best access:** [Royal Danish Library complete facsimile, transcription, and
  scholarly apparatus](https://poma.kb.dk/).
- **Why not mirrored here:** the official site exposes the complete 1,200-page
  autograph manuscript with page-level images, multilingual navigation, and
  editorial context. A monolithic local derivative would be less usable and
  less authoritative.
- **Caution:** primary for Guaman Poma's intervention and the traditions he
  records, not a transparent transcript of a uniform “Inca belief system.”

### Juan de Betanzos, *Suma y narración de los Incas* (written c. 1551)

- **Type:** early colonial chronicle based in part on Quechua testimony;
  primary ethnohistorical source in a later edition.
- **Local:** [plain text](documents/1551-betanzos-suma-y-narracion-de-los-incas.txt)
- **Source:** [Project Gutenberg edition and formats](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25705)
- **Coverage:** Inca origins, Pachacuti, imperial history, ritual, and conquest.
- **Caution:** Betanzos's position as interpreter and colonial official, his
  family relationship to Cuxirimay Ocllo, editorial history, and the partial
  manuscript known to early editors all matter.

### Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa, *Historia de los Incas* (1572)

- **Type:** colonial inquiry commissioned under Viceroy Toledo; primary for the
  inquiry and the testimonies it shaped, accessed through a later translation.
- **Local:** [1907 English Hakluyt Society edition](documents/1572-sarmiento-history-of-the-incas-1907-edition.pdf)
- **Source:** [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/historyofincas00sarm)
- **Coverage:** origin accounts, dynastic history, conquests, and Toledo's case
  against Inca sovereignty.
- **Caution:** the work had an explicit colonial-political purpose. Treat its
  harmonized chronology and “tyranny” framing critically.

### Joan de Santa Cruz Pachacuti Yamqui Salcamaygua, *Relación de antigüedades deste reyno del Pirú* (early 1600s)

- **Type:** Indigenous Andean colonial manuscript; primary ethnohistorical,
  linguistic, and diagrammatic source.
- **Local:** included in [*Tres relaciones de antigüedades peruanas*
  (1879)](documents/1879-tres-relaciones-antiguedades-peruanas.pdf).
- **Modern access:** [1993 IFEA critical edition record and online text](https://books.openedition.org/ifea/2333)
- **Manuscript record:** [Peruvian Ministry of Education repository description
  linking the Biblioteca Digital Hispánica manuscript](https://repocaslit.minedu.gob.pe/handle/123456789/122?show=full)
- **Coverage:** Inca origin/history, sacred geography, hymns, Quechua/Aymara
  passages, and the famous Coricancha cosmological drawing.
- **Caution:** use a modern critical transcription for linguistic or diagram
  analysis; the 1879 transcription contains known errors.

### Cristóbal de Molina and related accounts, *Narratives of the Rites and Laws of the Yncas* (1873 collection)

- **Type:** translations of several early colonial accounts, including Molina;
  primary texts in a nineteenth-century editorial frame.
- **Local:** [Hakluyt Society volume](documents/1873-markham-narratives-rites-laws-incas.pdf)
- **Source:** [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/narrativesofrite00markiala)
- **Coverage:** ceremonies, origin narratives, prayers, laws, and Pachacuti
  Yamqui material.
- **Caution:** Markham's translations, spellings, omissions, and theories need
  checking against manuscripts and modern critical editions.

### *Manuscrito de Huarochirí* / *Dioses y hombres de Huarochirí*

- **Type:** anonymous Indigenous Quechua-language colonial manuscript recording
  Huarochirí narratives and ritual traditions. The local item is a digitized
  selection from José María Arguedas's 1966 Quechua-Spanish edition. It contains
  the front matter and opening chapters, not the complete book.
- **Local:** [Arguedas edition, opening selection](documents/1966-arguedas-dioses-hombres-huarochiri.pdf)
- **Official source:** [Biblioteca Nacional del Perú digital
  item](https://bibliotecadigital.bnp.gob.pe/items/fc066874-fe3e-41fe-bf37-a0ce1ae3c2b1/full?origin=internal)
- **Manuscript shelfmark:** Biblioteca Nacional de España, MSS/3169.
- **Coverage:** Pariacaca, Huallallo Carhuincho, Cuniraya Viracocha,
  Chaupiñamca, local wak'as, sacred landscapes, ritual, and community histories.
- **Caution:** this is Huarochirí material produced amid campaigns against
  Indigenous religion, not a generic encyclopedia of “Inca mythology.” Compare
  translations and the original Quechua for close analysis.

### Pedro Cieza de León, *Crónica del Perú* (written 1540s; part I published 1553)

- **Type:** early travel, conquest, geographic, and ethnohistorical chronicle.
- **Local:** [1922 edition of part I](documents/1553-cieza-cronica-del-peru-1922-edition.pdf)
  and [1880 first edition of part II, *El señorío de los Incas*](documents/1880-cieza-segunda-parte-cronica-del-peru.pdf).
- **Sources:** [Internet Archive part I](https://archive.org/details/lacrnicadelper00ciez),
  [Internet Archive part II](https://archive.org/details/segundaparte00ciezrich), and
  [1553 first edition at Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-8392.html).
- **Coverage:** Colombia through Bolivia, Andean sites and peoples, roads,
  customs, traditions, Inca government, and Spanish invasion.
- **Caution:** mixes observation, interview, hearsay, classical comparison, and
  colonial categories.

### Francisco de Xerez and Miguel de Estete, *Verdadera relación de la conquista del Perú*

- **Type:** conquest narratives by Spanish participants; primary for invasion
  events and conquistador representation.
- **Local:** [1891 edition](documents/1891-xerez-estete-verdadera-relacion-conquista-peru.pdf)
- **Sources:** [Library of Congress public-domain item](https://www.loc.gov/item/03022598/)
  and [Internet Archive copy](https://archive.org/details/verdaderarelaci00xere).
- **Coverage:** Cajamarca, Atahualpa, Cusco, and the early invasion.
- **Caution:** direct participants had incentives to justify violence,
  royal claims, and distributions of wealth.

### José de Acosta, *Natural and Moral History of the Indies* (1590)

- **Type:** colonial natural history and ethnography; primary for late
  sixteenth-century synthesis and Jesuit intellectual frameworks.
- **Local:** [volume I](documents/1880-acosta-natural-moral-history-vol-1.pdf)
  and [volume II](documents/1880-acosta-natural-moral-history-vol-2.pdf), 1880
  Hakluyt Society translation.
- **Sources:** [volume I](https://archive.org/details/naturalmoralhist60acosrich),
  [volume II](https://archive.org/details/naturalmoralhist61acosrich).
- **Coverage:** environment, plants, peoples, Andean religion, government, and
  origin theories.
- **Caution:** a learned colonial synthesis, not an excavation report or an
  Indigenous-authored account.

## Early Amazon accounts and legendary-place documents

### Gaspar de Carvajal, account of Francisco de Orellana's Amazon voyage (1541–1542)

- **Type:** participant narrative; primary expedition and ethnohistorical source
  preserved in a later edition.
- **Local:** [1894 *Descubrimiento del río de las Amazonas*](documents/1542-carvajal-descubrimiento-rio-amazonas-1894-edition.pdf)
- **Source:** [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/descubrimientod00carvgoog)
- **Coverage:** river travel, settlements, warfare, food systems, political
  organization, and the women-warriors report later tied to the river's name.
- **Caution:** expedition stress, hostile encounters, translation problems,
  hearsay, and classical “Amazon” expectations shape the text. Archaeology may
  test settlement claims, not validate every description.

### Cristóbal de Acuña, *Nuevo descubrimiento del gran río de las Amazonas* (1641)

- **Type:** Jesuit eyewitness/official report from Pedro Teixeira's return
  voyage; primary colonial geographic and ethnohistorical source.
- **Local:** [1641 edition](documents/1641-acuna-nuevo-descubrimiento-gran-rio-amazonas.pdf)
- **Source:** [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/nuevodescubrimie00acua)
- **Coverage:** river geography, named peoples, settlements, resources, and
  colonial possibilities.
- **Caution:** produced within Iberian imperial competition and missionary
  expansion.

### Manuscript 512 (1753/1754)

- **Type:** genuine colonial archival manuscript reporting a supposed ruined
  city; primary evidence for the report and later legend, not for the city's
  existence.
- **Best record:** [Biblioteca Nacional do Brasil catalog with digitization
  link and shelf data](https://acervo.bn.gov.br/sophia_web/acervo/detalhe/1463648)
- **Institutional context:** [Biblioteca Nacional feature on Documento
  512](https://antigo.bn.gov.br/en/node/1815)
- **Backup images:** [Wikimedia Commons file/category entry](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Manuscrito_512_01.jpg)
- **Coverage:** a bandeirante-style ruined-city narrative later connected with
  Phoenicians, El Dorado, and Fawcett's Z.
- **Caution:** archival authenticity and narrative veracity are distinct.

## Expeditions and early archaeological syntheses

### Hiram Bingham, *Inca Land* (1922)

- **Type:** expedition narrative and early archaeological description.
- **Local:** [plain text](documents/1922-bingham-inca-land.txt)
- **Source:** [Project Gutenberg / Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/incaland10772gut)
- **Coverage:** Cusco region, Vitcos, Vilcabamba, Machu Picchu, and expedition
  logistics.
- **Caution:** “discovery” rhetoric minimizes local residents and knowledge;
  identifications evolved after publication.

### George Earl Church, *Aborigines of South America* (1912)

- **Type:** early continental synthesis with historical and geographical
  observations.
- **Local:** [scanned volume](documents/1912-church-aborigines-of-south-america.pdf)
- **Source:** [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/aboriginesofsout00churuoft)
- **Caution:** historically useful for tracing old theories, terminology, and
  source citations; not a current synthesis.

### Erland Nordenskiöld, *The Copper and Bronze Ages in South America* (1921)

- **Type:** early archaeological synthesis and artifact study.
- **Local:** [scanned volume](documents/1921-nordenskiold-copper-bronze-ages-south-america.pdf)
- **Source:** [Internet Archive item](https://archive.org/details/copperbronzeages00nordrich)
- **Caution:** valuable comparative documentation in an outdated culture-history
  frame.

### João Barbosa Rodrigues, *Antiguidades do Amazonas* (1870s–1890s)

- **Type:** early Amazonian archaeological/ethnographic observations; primary
  for collections, sites, and nineteenth-century interpretation.
- **Official records:** [Biblioteca Virtual do Amazonas](https://omeka.cultura.am.gov.br/s/bv/item/23729)
  and [Brazilian Federal Senate rare-book record](https://www2.senado.leg.br/bdsf/handle/id/242810?show=full).
- **Local:** [*Ensaios de sciencia*, volume 2, containing *Antiguidades do
  Amazonas: a arte cerâmica*](documents/1876-barbosa-rodrigues-antiguidades-amazonas-vol-2.pdf).
- **Open scan:** [Wikimedia-hosted *Ensaios de sciencia*, volume
  2](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Ensaios_de_sciencia_%28Vol._2b%29.pdf).
- **Coverage:** ceramics, inscriptions, burial mounds, stone tools, and
  ethnographic comparisons.
- **Caution:** pioneering observations coexist with nineteenth-century racial,
  diffusionist, and classificatory assumptions.

## Foundational excavation and survey reports

### Betty J. Meggers and Clifford Evans, *Archeological Investigations at the Mouth of the Amazon* (1957)

- **Type:** major survey, excavation, ceramic chronology, and collections report.
- **Official access:** [Smithsonian repository record and 51 MB
  PDF](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/15461).
- **Coverage:** Marajó, Amapá, Caviana, Maracá, Mazagão, phases and sequences,
  plates, maps, and excavation data.
- **Caution:** foundational data and environmental-determinist conclusions must
  be evaluated separately.

### Clifford Evans and Betty J. Meggers, *Archaeological Investigations on the Rio Napo, Eastern Ecuador* (1968)

- **Type:** field survey, excavation, and ceramic sequence report.
- **Official access:** [Smithsonian repository record, PDF/EPUB, and
  DOI](https://repository.si.edu/handle/10088/1353).
- **Coverage:** Napo, Tiputini, Yasuní, phase definitions, collections, maps,
  radiocarbon dates, and proposed regional connections.
- **Caution:** compare diffusion/migration conclusions with newer dates and
  archaeological models.

## Modern strong secondary and field-science sources

### Prümers et al. 2022: Casarabe lidar

- **Local:** [open-access Nature article](documents/2022-prumers-casarabe-low-density-urbanism.pdf)
- **Canonical:** [Nature article and supplements](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04780-4)
- **Finding:** lidar documents large centers and a four-tier settlement system
  in the Bolivian Amazon. `strong-secondary / primary remote-sensing data`

### Rostain et al. 2024: Upano Valley urbanism

- **Local:** [open supplementary materials](documents/2024-rostain-upano-garden-urbanism-supplement.pdf)
- **Canonical:** [Science DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi6317)
- **Finding:** lidar and excavation support extensive, early garden urbanism in
  Amazonian Ecuador. `strong-secondary / primary field and lidar data`

### Watling et al. 2017: Acre geoglyphs and forest history

- **Local:** [accepted manuscript](documents/2017-watling-acre-geoglyph-builders.pdf)
- **Canonical:** [PNAS DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1614359114)
- **Finding:** geoglyph builders managed forests for millennia without the
  regional-scale clearance of the modern era. `strong-secondary / primary paleoecological data`

### de Souza et al. 2018: southern Amazon earthworks

- **Local:** [open-access Nature Communications article](documents/2018-desouza-southern-amazon-earth-builders.pdf)
- **Canonical:** [journal article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-03510-7)
- **Finding:** predictive modelling and field validation indicate settlement by
  earth-building cultures across the southern Amazon rim.
  `strong-secondary / primary survey data`

### Schaan, Pärssinen, Ranzi, and Piccoli 2007: western Amazon geoglyphs

- **Bibliographic lead:** *Revista de Arqueologia* 20, 67–82; the [Fundação
  Joaquim Nabuco research guide](https://pesquisaescolar.fundaj.gov.br/pt-br/artigo/geoglifos/)
  provides the full citation and related Portuguese bibliography.
- **Finding:** early synthesis of Acre earthworks as evidence for complex social
  organization. `strong-secondary`

## High-value sources still to acquire or link more precisely

- Complete, modern critical editions of Betanzos and Titu Cusi Yupanqui.
- A complete *Manuscrito de Huarochirí* in an authoritative Quechua
  transcription and modern translation. The local Arguedas scan contains only
  the opening portion.
- Curt Nimuendajú's archaeological notes, maps, and lower-Amazon collections.
- Modern excavation monographs for Santarém/Tapajó, Marajó, Kuhikugu/Upper
  Xingu, Upano, Casarabe, Chiribiquete, and the Guianas.
- National heritage inventories and site reports from IPHAN (Brazil), Peru's
  Ministry of Culture, Bolivia's heritage authorities, ICANH (Colombia), and
  counterparts in Ecuador, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname, and French Guiana.
- Indigenous-authored or community-governed publications and digital archives,
  with access and sacred-knowledge protocols respected.
