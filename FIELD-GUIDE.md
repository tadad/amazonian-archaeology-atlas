# Broad Field Guide

This is the curated orientation layer for the much larger machine-discovered
catalog in [`topics/`](topics/). It identifies the principal research clusters,
the likely evidentiary posture, and the most productive trails for later deep
dives. Labels apply to the named subject broadly, not to every claim ever made
about it.

The [source library](sources/INDEX.md) connects these clusters to downloaded
primary texts, field reports, and open-access studies.

## Amazonian archaeology and historical ecology

### High-priority, archaeologically corroborated clusters

- **Casarabe culture and the Llanos de Mojos, Bolivia**: monumental platform
  settlements, causeways, canals, reservoirs, raised fields, and a tiered
  settlement system. The 2022 lidar work on Cotoca and Landívar is a major
  corrective to “empty Amazon” narratives. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Upano Valley, Ecuador**: Kilamope and Sangay settlement complexes with
  platforms, roads, fields, and drainage; recent lidar mapping demonstrates an
  extensive, early engineered landscape. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Upper Xingu / Kuhikugu, Brazil**: plaza settlements, roads, bridges,
  ditches, and managed landscapes associated with ancestors of present-day
  Xinguano peoples. Community knowledge and archaeology should be read
  together. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Acre geoglyphs, Brazil**: hundreds of geometric ditched enclosures and
  related forest-management evidence. Their functions and settlement context
  remain research questions; they are not South American “Nazca lines.”
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Southern Amazon rim earthworks**: ditched enclosures, mound villages, road
  systems, and other construction across portions of Acre, Rondônia, Mato
  Grosso, and Bolivia. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Marajó Island / Marajoara culture, Brazil**: mounds or *tesos*, habitation
  deposits, urns, elaborate ceramics, and managed floodplain landscapes.
  Population scale and political organization have a long interpretive
  history. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Tapajó/Santarém culture, Brazil**: dense archaeological deposits, ceramic
  traditions, anthropomorphic vessels, muiraquitãs, and ethnohistoric accounts
  around the lower Tapajós. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Amazonian Dark Earth / terra preta**: anthropogenic soils and settlement
  legacies distributed across the basin. Formation histories vary by place and
  should not be reduced to a single technology or culture.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Amazonian Polychrome Tradition**: a broad archaeological ceramic horizon,
  including Marajoara, Guarita, and Napo-related complexes. Treat culture,
  language, migration, and ceramic style as separate propositions.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Napo River archaeological sequence, Ecuador/Peru**: excavated ceramic
  phases and settlements along an important upper-Amazon corridor. The 1968
  Meggers and Evans report is foundational but its diffusionist framework must
  be compared with later work. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Pedra Pintada / Monte Alegre, Brazil**: rockshelter occupations and rock
  art associated with early Holocene settlement evidence in the lower Amazon.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Serra da Capivara and Pedra Furada, Brazil**: abundant rock art and secure
  Holocene archaeology; the proposed much earlier human dates remain
  controversial and must be separated from the undisputed later record.
  `mixed/disputed`
- **Calçoene / Rego Grande megalithic complex, Amapá**: stone settings and
  associated funerary archaeology, popularly called an “Amazon Stonehenge.”
  The archaeology is real; sensational comparisons add little.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Maracá funerary complexes, Amapá**: caves and distinctive anthropomorphic
  funerary urns documented since the nineteenth century.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Aristé and Koriabo archaeological traditions, Guianas/Amapá**: ceramic and
  settlement sequences important for linking the Guianas with the lower Amazon
  and Caribbean-facing networks. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Hertenrits and coastal raised-field settlements, Suriname/French Guiana**:
  mounds, fields, canals, and coastal adaptation. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Chiribiquete rock art, Colombia**: enormous, long-lived rock-art complex in
  the Colombian Amazon. Dating and interpretation are active research areas;
  avoid projecting a single narrative onto all panels.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Orinoco/Atures rock-art and earthwork landscapes, Venezuela/Colombia**:
  petroglyphs, settlement archaeology, and river-corridor interaction.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`

### Foundational debates worth tracking

- **“Pristine wilderness” versus cultural landscape**: now superseded as a
  simple binary. Human modification was extensive in many places and limited in
  others; scale and chronology require local evidence.
- **Population before 1492**: estimates remain model-dependent. Settlement
  archaeology, soils, botanical histories, and colonial demographic collapse
  must be integrated without converting a basin-wide range into a fact.
- **Environmental limitation versus social complexity**: Betty Meggers and
  Clifford Evans were foundational field archaeologists, but strong
  environmental-determinist and diffusionist interpretations in their work are
  not current consensus.
- **Language, ceramics, and migration**: proposed connections involving
  Arawakan, Tupian, and Cariban expansions are productive but cannot be inferred
  from pottery style alone.
- **Indigenous historical knowledge**: should be treated as knowledge with
  authorship, custodianship, and context, not as an anonymous quarry for myths
  that archaeologists later “verify.”

## Andes, Peru, Bolivia, and Inca-connected frontiers

### Peru and the central Andes

- **Norte Chico / Caral-Supe**: early monumental centers and complex society
  on Peru's north-central coast. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Huaca Prieta and coastal preceramic sites**: early subsistence, textiles,
  architecture, and maritime/plant economies. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Sechín, Chankillo, and Casma Valley complexes**: monumental architecture,
  imagery, and the well-supported solar-observatory interpretation at Chankillo.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Chavín de Huántar and Chavín interaction sphere**: monumental ritual center
  and far-reaching material style; “mother culture” language should be handled
  cautiously. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Cupisnique**: north-coast archaeological tradition with relationships to
  Chavín that remain interpretive rather than a simple identity.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Paracas**: cemeteries, textiles, cranial modification, and regional
  archaeology. Claims that modified skulls are nonhuman are pseudoscience.
  `archaeologically-corroborated; attached alien claims debunked`
- **Nazca culture, Cahuachi, and Nazca Lines**: settlements, pilgrimage/ritual
  center, ceramics, trophy-head debates, and geoglyph landscapes. Alien or
  airfield claims are pseudoscientific. `mixed`
- **Moche and Sipán**: monumental centers, elite tombs, iconography, warfare,
  sacrifice, and multiple regional polities. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Wari**: expansive Middle Horizon state or empire with provincial centers,
  roads, and administrative architecture; relationship with Tiwanaku remains a
  major research subject. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Chimú and Chan Chan**: north-coast state and adobe capital preceding Inca
  conquest. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Chachapoya, Kuélap, Gran Pajatén, and Revash**: eastern Andean societies and
  sites often romanticized as “cloud warriors.” Separate archaeology from late
  colonial ethnic generalization. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Pachacámac**: long-lived coastal pilgrimage center incorporated into the
  Inca sphere. `archaeologically-corroborated; ethnohistorically-documented`
- **Cusco, Coricancha, Sacsayhuamán, and the ceque system**: material and
  ethnohistorical center of Inca imperial organization and ritual geography.
  `mixed archaeological/ethnohistorical`
- **Machu Picchu**: Inca royal/elite estate and ritual landscape; “lost city”
  framing erases continuous local knowledge. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Choquequirao**: Inca center with terraces, plazas, and distinctive llama
  mosaics. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Vilcabamba and Espíritu Pampa**: the Neo-Inca capital was historically
  attested and archaeologically identified; it should not be conflated with
  every Paititi claim. `archaeologically-corroborated/historically-attested`
- **Qhapaq Ñan and tambos**: imperial road, bridge, storage, and administrative
  systems across the Andes. `archaeologically-corroborated`

### Bolivia and the south-central Andes

- **Tiwanaku and Pumapunku**: major state center and monumental precinct near
  Lake Titicaca. Precision stonework is human technology; ancient-alien claims
  are pseudoscientific. `archaeologically-corroborated; attached claims debunked`
- **Chiripa**: formative-period Titicaca Basin settlement tradition.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Samaipata / El Fuerte**: multi-period site with pre-Inca and Inca use on an
  Andean-lowland interface. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Iskanwaya and Mollo culture**: substantial settlement in Bolivia's eastern
  cordillera. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Incallajta**: large Inca administrative and frontier center.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`

### Inca southern frontier and “Patagonia” caution

The securely demonstrated Inca sphere reached northwestern Argentina and
central Chile, not Patagonia in the broad modern sense. “Inca Patagonia” should
therefore be treated as a frontier/contact research question, not an assumed
imperial province.

- **El Shincal de Quimivil, Argentina**: major Inca administrative center.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Santa Rosa de Tastil and Quebrada del Toro, Argentina**: large pre-Inca
  settlement affected by Inca reorganization. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Pucará de Chena, Cerro La Muralla, and central Chile frontier sites**:
  fortifications and Inca/local material on a contested frontier.
  `archaeologically-corroborated; interpretations debated`
- **High-altitude capacocha shrines**: ritual deposits and child burials on
  Andean summits in Peru, Bolivia, Chile, and Argentina.
  `archaeologically-corroborated/ethnohistorically-documented`
- **City of the Caesars / Ciudad de los Césares**: a colonial legendary city
  shifted around Patagonia in successive retellings; not an Inca site.
  `legendary`

## Northern Andes and Amazon-facing regions

- **Valdivia, Machalilla, and Chorrera, Ecuador**: early ceramic and formative
  traditions; old trans-Pacific claims require very critical review.
  `archaeologically-corroborated; diffusion claims disputed`
- **La Tolita–Tumaco**: Pacific Ecuador/Colombia archaeological tradition known
  for metalwork and figurines. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Cochasquí and Caranqui, Ecuador**: monumental earthen platforms, regional
  polities, and Inca encounter. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Ingapirca and Tomebamba, Ecuador**: Inca imperial sites with Cañari
  histories. `archaeologically-corroborated/historically-attested`
- **San Agustín and Tierradentro, Colombia**: monumental sculpture, mounds, and
  hypogea. `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Teyuna / Ciudad Perdida, Colombia**: Tairona urban center known to local
  Indigenous communities before its outsider “discovery.”
  `archaeologically-corroborated`
- **Muisca, Lake Guatavita, and the Muisca raft**: the documented offering
  tradition helped generate El Dorado narratives; a real ritual complex does
  not corroborate a golden city. `mixed`
- **Taima-Taima, Venezuela**: late Pleistocene archaeological locality central
  to debates over early settlement. `archaeologically-corroborated; dating and associations debated`
- **Lake Valencia/Valencioid archaeology, Venezuela**: settlement and ceramic
  traditions relevant to Caribbean-lowland interaction.
  `archaeologically-corroborated`

## Legends, oral traditions, and lost-place complexes

- **Paititi / Gran Paititi**: changing traditions about an Inca refuge or rich
  lowland place east of the Andes; sometimes entangled with real Neo-Inca
  history, mission records, and modern expeditions. No identified “Paititi city”
  is archaeologically corroborated. `legendary/disputed`
- **El Dorado**: began in European retellings of a person/ritual associated
  with Muisca offerings and expanded into a mobile golden kingdom/city across
  northern South America and the Guianas. `mixed historical-root/legendary-city`
- **Manoa and Lake Parime**: cartographic and colonial elaborations of the El
  Dorado complex, often placed in Guiana. `legendary`
- **Lost City of Z**: Percy Fawcett's hypothesized complex society in Brazil,
  influenced by colonial documents and his own theories. Modern Amazonian
  archaeology vindicates neither his exact city nor all his assumptions.
  `historically-attested expedition/disputed target`
- **Manuscript 512**: genuine eighteenth-century archival document reporting a
  supposed ruined city in Brazil; authenticity as a document does not verify
  its story. `historically-attested document/legendary claim`
- **Akakor**: twentieth-century story promoted by Karl Brugger from claims by
  “Tatunca Nara”; associated historical claims lack credible corroboration.
  `debunked-or-pseudoscientific`
- **Kandire / Candire**: Guaraní and Chiriguano promised-land complex entangled
  with colonial searches, migration histories, and Spanish treasure motives.
  `ethnohistorically-documented/legendary`
- **Inkarri**: post-conquest Andean tradition of the dismembered Inca whose
  return/restoration remains expected. `ethnohistorically-documented`
- **Ayar siblings, Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo, Tampu Tocco, Pacaritambo**:
  multiple Inca origin traditions preserved through colonial authors with
  political and local variants. `ethnohistorically-documented/legendary`
- **Viracocha, Pachacámac, and Andean creation cycles**: colonial records,
  material cult places, and later harmonizations need to be disentangled.
  `mixed`
- **Yuruparí/Jurupari**: northwestern Amazon ceremonial and narrative complex,
  recorded in Indigenous languages and outsider ethnographies. Community and
  sacred-context constraints matter. `ethnohistorically-documented`
- **Mapinguari**: Amazonian forest-being traditions, later reframed in popular
  cryptozoology. The tradition is documented; giant-ground-sloth survival is
  uncorroborated. `legendary; cryptozoological claim disputed`
- **Curupira, Caipora, Iara, Cobra Grande/Boiúna, Matinta Pereira**: diverse
  Brazilian and Amazonian traditions with Indigenous, Afro-Brazilian, and
  colonial layers. `ethnohistorically-documented/legendary`
- **Amazon warrior-women and the naming of the river**: Carvajal's account is
  primary evidence for the expedition's report and European classical framing,
  not sufficient proof of a polity of classical “Amazons.”
  `historically-attested report/disputed interpretation`

## Claims to quarantine, not silently discard

These belong in the corpus because they affect public understanding and often
attach themselves to real sites. They should never inherit credibility from the
archaeology they appropriate.

- Ancient aliens at Nazca, Tiwanaku/Pumapunku, Sacsayhuamán, or Machu Picchu.
- Nonhuman/alien claims about Paracas modified skulls.
- Phoenician, Hebrew, Egyptian, or Atlantean readings of Pedra do Ingá and other
  South American petroglyphs without secure epigraphic context.
- Akakor as an ancient subterranean empire.
- “Ratanabá” as a 450-million-year-old Amazonian city.
- Conflation of recent lidar discoveries with proof of El Dorado, Paititi, Z,
  Atlantis, or a single basin-wide empire.
- Dates for human occupation that exceed the age of *Homo sapiens* or treat
  geological formations as architecture.
