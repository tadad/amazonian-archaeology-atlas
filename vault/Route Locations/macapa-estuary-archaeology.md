# Macapá estuary archaeology

## Why it is on this map

Macapá is a major modern settlement near the downstream end of the route and anchors a substantial archaeological record on the estuarine coast.

## Expedition event

In this broad estuarine phase, the narrative describes inhabited islands, tidal channels, vessel damage, attacks, and extended repairs ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 117|PDF p. 117]]). A separate expedition marker represents that uncertain event cluster; Macapá remains the primary archaeology node.

## Archaeology

Frazão and colleagues synthesize seven small or shallow sites interpreted as seasonal components within a broader settlement system of villages, cemeteries, camps, and activity areas ([[Papers/2024-frazao-et-al-amapa-seasonal-sites|Frazão et al. 2024]]). AP-MA-05 adds dense ceramics, funerary urns, and anthropogenic soils in the modern city ([[Papers/2022-xavier-et-al-ap-ma-05|Xavier, Costa, and Xavier 2022]]).

## Caution

The public city point does not claim that Orellana stopped at modern Macapá, identify an exact 1542 channel, or publish the coordinates of individual sites.
