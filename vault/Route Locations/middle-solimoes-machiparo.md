# Middle Solimões / Machiparo

## Expedition event

The narrative describes Machiparo as densely settled and says the expedition encountered armed canoe forces ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 106|PDF p. 106]]). It continues with a running battle, food seizure, damp gunpowder, many wounded, Pedro de Ampudia's death, and a force it numbers at 130 canoes and 8,000 fighters ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 108|PDF p. 108]]). Those numbers belong to the account and are not an archaeological population estimate.

## Archaeology

Modern work documents extensive riverine occupations, Polychrome ceramics, and regional exchange. The São João settlement near the Caiambé system is one relevant example ([[Papers/2021-lopes-cronicas-medio-solimoes#Page 9|Lopes 2021, PDF p. 9]]), but the same study cautions against identifying a single site directly with one passage in a chronicle.

Archaeometric work on Conjunto Vilas and São João adds material characterization of Pocó, Tefé, and Caiambé ceramic assemblages ([[Papers/2020-oliveira-et-al-conjunto-vilas-sao-joao#Page 1|Oliveira et al. 2020, PDF p. 1]]). Tamanaha's dissertation extends the comparative sequence downstream through Coari and the Solimões–Negro confluence ([[Papers/2012-tamanaha-ocupacao-policroma-solimoes|Tamanaha 2012]]).

Lopes and colleagues place the historical geography called Machiparo from around the Japurá mouth to before the Coari mouth ([[Papers/2024-lopes-et-al-medio-solimoes#Page 8|2024, PDF p. 8]]). The marker generalizes that research area and does not disclose a protected site coordinate.
