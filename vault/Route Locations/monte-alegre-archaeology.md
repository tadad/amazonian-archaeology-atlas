# Monte Alegre archaeological landscape

## Why it is on this map

Monte Alegre is a modern route settlement associated with an unusually deep archaeological record. The city marker groups regional research without publishing cave or open-air-site coordinates.

## Expedition event

Monte Alegre falls within the downstream span of modern reconstructions of the narrative's São João province. The account describes very closely spaced villages, fighting, large inhabited islands, and many piraguas ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 114|PDF p. 114]]). The modern research node does not identify one reported settlement.

## Archaeology

Pedra Pintada preserves a late-Pleistocene occupation documented through stratigraphy, radiocarbon and luminescence dates, lithics, plant remains, fauna, and rock art ([[Papers/1996-roosevelt-et-al-pedra-pintada|Roosevelt et al. 1996]]). Open-air sites add a much later ceramic sequence beginning around the twelfth century, with local, Santarém, Konduri, and Koriabo-related elements ([[Papers/2016-barreto-nascimento-monte-alegre-ceramics|Barreto and Nascimento 2016]]).

## Caution

Only the late ceramic sequence overlaps the broad period relevant to 1542. The deep chronology is shown because it defines the research landscape, not because Paleoindian occupation explains the expedition report.
