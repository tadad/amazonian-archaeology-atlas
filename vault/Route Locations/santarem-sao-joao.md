# Santarém / São João province

## Expedition event

The narrative describes dense villages, fighting, and a long province it calls São João ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 114|PDF p. 114]]). Lima, Moraes, and Sá place that reported province between modern Juruti and Prainha and propose the central area of modern Santarém as its sixteenth-century political center ([[Papers/2020-lima-moraes-sa-tapajonico#Page 2|PDF p. 2]]).

## Modern archaeology

The same study analyzes Tapajonic perforated discs and situates them in a rich archaeological record of specialized production and exchange at the Tapajós–Amazon confluence. These material contexts give the atlas a concrete modern research base without treating every detail of the colonial narrative as confirmed.

Urban fieldwork has since defined two exceptionally large precolonial settlement areas beneath modern Santarém. The Aldeia and Porto sites cover approximately 121 and 89 hectares and were contemporaneous around AD 1200–1600 ([[Papers/2025-gomes-urban-archaeology-santarem|Gomes 2025]]). This is the atlas's strongest site-scale comparison for the chronicle's account of large lower-Amazon settlements, while remaining independent evidence rather than automatic confirmation of the reported province.
