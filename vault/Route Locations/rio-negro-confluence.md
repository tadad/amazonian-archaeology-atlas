# Rio Negro confluence

## Expedition event

The expedition narrative passes a major black-water river amid descriptions of long settled stretches. Markham's note identifies this tributary as the Rio Negro ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 110|PDF p. 110]]).

## Caution

The modern confluence is a known geographic landmark. The historical association comes from the route sequence and an editor's identification, not a coordinate recorded by the expedition.

## Archaeology

Central Amazon Project research establishes a ceramic chronology relating Açutuba, Manacapuru, and Paredão occupations around the confluence ([[Papers/2011-lima-neves-incised-rim-central-amazon|Lima and Neves 2011]]). Tamanaha adds Guarita-phase research extending between Coari and the confluence, including occupations continuing toward the sixteenth century ([[Papers/2012-tamanaha-ocupacao-policroma-solimoes|Tamanaha 2012]]).
