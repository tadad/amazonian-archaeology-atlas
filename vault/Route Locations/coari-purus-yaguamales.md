# Coari–Purus / Yaguamales

## Why it is approximate

Lopes and colleagues summarize a critical historical reconstruction that places Yaguamales between the Coari and Purus mouths ([[Papers/2024-lopes-et-al-medio-solimoes#Page 8|PDF p. 8]]). The marker uses Coari only as a recognizable western anchor for that long corridor.

## Expedition event

After leaving Machiparo, the narrative describes a tributary called the River of Trinity, fertile inhabited islands, and a village with elaborately painted ceramics, a country house, and palm-wood figures. It also records unverified claims about gold and silver ([[Papers/1859-markham-orellana-voyage#Page 109|PDF p. 109]]). This broad Coari–Purus marker does not identify the tributary or village with an archaeological site.

## Archaeology

The same study compares documents describing abundant cultivation and storage with archaeological evidence for late precolonial Polychrome occupations across the Middle Solimões. It treats province names and chroniclers' descriptions critically rather than as direct ethnic or site identifications.

Tamanaha's Coari research analyzes Guarita-phase settlement chronology, ceramic assemblages, artifact density, and spatial organization. The repository abstract places the latest occupations into the sixteenth century, providing a particularly relevant archaeological comparison for the expedition period ([[Papers/2012-tamanaha-ocupacao-policroma-solimoes|Tamanaha 2012]]).
